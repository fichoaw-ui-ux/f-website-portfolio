<PartHeader number="01" title="Project Snapshot" />

<Section title="Background">
Jack Finance is a business financial services provider offering integrated financial management solutions. One of its main services is ***Local Transfer***, built to help companies manage domestic payments in one place.

Jack runs on two platforms: **Jack Portal (web)** and the **Jack Mobile App**. But their roles weren't balanced. Every *Local Transfer* could only be created in the Portal.

Every transaction at Jack passes through three roles: the **Drafter** who creates it, the **Approver** who approves it, and the **Releaser** who releases the funds from the company balance. For *Local Transfer*, the Mobile App only served Approvers and Releasers — the Drafter, the person who starts every transaction, still had to open the Portal. The only thing a Drafter could create from their phone was a *reimbursement*.

This case study covers how *Local Transfer* came to the Jack Mobile App — not by shrinking the Portal version, but by redesigning the flow around how people actually use their phones.
</Section>

<PartHeader number="02" title="Context & Problem" />

<Section title="A. Context Problem">
The first signal was low Mobile App usage. Monthly active users across every mobile module sat at just **31–50 users** a month (April–September 2023). The reason was clear: *drafters* — the people who create transactions — had no reason to open the app.

| Month (2023) | Unique Users |
| --- | --- |
| April | 44 |
| May | 50 |
| June | 47 |
| July | 39 |
| August | 31 |
| September | 41 |

**Fira (my PM)** then found data that made it clear who was losing out the most. From an analysis of transaction patterns from January 2023 to February 2024:

> **72% of Local Transfer batches on Jack Portal held only one transaction.**

In other words, many users — especially SME owners and founders — only needed to send one payment. But to do it, they still had to sit at a laptop and go through a flow built for bulk payments.

Fira backed this up by reviewing user feedback and running short interviews with a few clients and internal stakeholders. The result was consistent: **having to use a desktop for a quick, single payment felt like a hassle**.
</Section>

<Section title="B. Current Experience">
Picture a Drafter out of the office who needs to pay a supplier that same day. They open the Jack Mobile App — but there's no button to create a new transaction. The only **Create New** button on Home sits on the *Reimbursement* card. So they have to wait until they can open a laptop, then go through the Portal's *Local Transfer* flow, built for many recipients, even though they're only sending to one person.

> **Not on mobile** — the Jack Mobile App had no Local Transfer feature.
>
> **Portal only** — the only way: open a laptop and go through the batch flow, even for a single recipient.

The image and prototype below show what creating a Local Transfer in the Portal looked like. Notice how many steps it takes, even for one recipient.
</Section>

Along this journey, two points held Drafters back the most — both are covered next.

<Section title="C. The Friction Points">
Those two points became **two friction points** that had to be solved together if the feature was going to get used.

**1. Local Transfer didn't exist on mobile.**

In the Jack Mobile App, the only transaction a Drafter could create was a *reimbursement*, through the *Create New* button on the Reimbursement card. There was no Local Transfer feature, and no general Create button that could lead to one.
</Section>

<ImageFull indent mobile src="/images/cs-local-transfer-mobile/placeholder.svg" alt="Jack Mobile App Home with Create New only on the Reimbursement card" caption="Friction #1 — The only button to create a transaction on Home: Create New on the Reimbursement card." />

<Section title=" " indent>
As a result, for urgent payments like paying a supplier or covering an ad-hoc expense, a Drafter out of the office had to wait until they could open a laptop. Business owners and finance managers who work from their phones every day had no other option.

**2. The Portal treated every transfer as a batch.**

The Portal flow always started as a batch — a batch name, a table or file upload, then bulk validation. Yet 72% of batches held only one recipient.
</Section>

  <ImageFull src="/images/cs-local-transfer-mobile/placeholder.svg" alt="Batch name screen in Jack Portal" caption="A batch name up front." />
  <ImageFull src="/images/cs-local-transfer-mobile/placeholder.svg" alt="Table or Excel upload screen in Jack Portal" caption="A table or Excel upload, no form." />
  <ImageFull src="/images/cs-local-transfer-mobile/placeholder.svg" alt="Bulk validation screen in Jack Portal" caption="Bulk validation." />
</ImageGrid>

<Section title=" " indent>
On a phone, that load is felt even more — a batch-first flow can't be carried over to mobile as it is.
</Section>

<Section title="D. Design Opportunity">
With these two friction points, the challenge wasn't just "move the feature to mobile". The question that started the design:

> **How do you make Local Transfer feel natural on a phone for a single transfer — without closing the door on people who still need batch?**

This wasn't just about moving a feature from the Portal to the phone. The two friction points were tied together: Drafters needed a path on mobile, but the existing flow was built for batch and couldn't be carried over as it was. If the Portal flow was simply shrunk, the feature would exist on mobile, but still be a hassle for the 72% of cases that only need one recipient.

On the other hand, if batch was dropped entirely, users who occasionally send to several people would be forced back to their laptops. The challenge was to design one flow that's fast for a single transfer — but can still grow into a batch without starting over.
</Section>

<PartHeader number="03" title="Design Decisions" />

<Section title="Kickoff">
After Fira wrapped up *discovery* — the data was mapped out and the problem statement was clear — I joined as the UI Designer. My work ran from exploring user flows and iterating on the UI to usability testing and handoff to mobile engineering.
</Section>

<Section title="Exploration & Usability Testing">
I designed **three end-to-end flow iterations**. Each one was a complete answer to both friction points — they differed in two things: **the order of steps**, and **when batch appears**.

- **Iteration 1** — Pick the recipient first. Batch is decided up front with checkboxes; the number of recipients checked decides single or batch.
- **Iteration 2** — The same order as the Portal: set the destination and transaction details first.
- **Iteration 3** — Pick the recipient first. Single is the default; batch opens later through an add-recipient icon.
</Section>

<Section title=" ">
All three iterations were tested with **10 internal Jack/Transfez users** — 5 already familiar with the Portal's Local Transfer flow, 5 not. The goal was to find out which flow was preferred and easiest to understand.

> **6 out of 10 users picked Iteration 3.** 3 users picked Iteration 1, and 1 user picked Iteration 2.

These results were then read from two angles — the order of steps and when batch appears. Each one shaped a design decision that answers one friction point.
</Section>

<Section title="A. A Path for Drafters on Mobile — From Portal-only to Native on the Phone">
**Friction addressed:** #1 — Local Transfer didn't exist on mobile.
</Section>

<Section title="A.1 Entry Point — From the Reimbursement Card to a Create Button" indent>
The first step was giving Drafters a way in. Before, creating a transaction on Home was only possible through the Reimbursement card. Now there's a **Create** button in the middle of the bottom navigation, which opens a choice of transaction types — including Local Transfer.
</Section>

</ImageGrid>

<Section title="A.2 Flow — From the Portal’s Order to “Pick the Recipient First”" indent>
But an entry point alone wasn't enough. The next question: should the steps follow the Portal, or follow the pattern of banking and e-wallet apps?

| | Portal order (Iteration 2) | Banking & e-wallet pattern (Iterations 1 & 3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single | Transfer details → Recipient | Recipient → Transfer details |
| Batch | Batch details → Recipient → Transfer details | Recipient → Transfer details → Batch details |
| Picked by | **1 user** | **9 users** |

Users picked the pattern they already knew from banking and e-wallet apps.

Even some users who were familiar with the Portal picked this pattern. **Consistency with phone habits turned out to matter more than consistency with the Portal.**
</Section>

<Section title="B. The Batch Load — From Batch-first to Single-first">
**Friction addressed:** #2 — The Portal treated every transfer as a batch.

The Portal's batch load couldn't come along to the phone — 72% of batches held only one transaction. But batch couldn't disappear either.

Iterations 1 and 3 both start from the recipient (Decision A). The difference is **when batch appears**: in Iteration 1, batch is decided up front with checkboxes; in Iteration 3, single is the default and batch only opens through the add-recipient icon.
</Section>

<FigmaEmbed indent url="https://www.figma.com/proto/ymvw1daaIRX7ZnVo9vGxmO/Local-Transfer?node-id=3829-133367&viewport=161%2C19%2C0.05&t=gG3FZ9d28lRsHvml-1&scaling=scale-down&content-scaling=fixed&starting-point-node-id=3829%3A133367&page-id=343%3A14094" title="Iteration 3 — batch" caption="Iteration 3 — batch opened through the add-recipient icon." />

<Section title=" " indent>
Of those 9 users, **6 picked Iteration 3** because the split between single and batch was easier to understand. A single transfer carries no batch steps at all, and batch is still there when it's needed.

*Batch became a branch of the single flow, not a separate path from the start.*
</Section>

<Section title="Recap: Two Final Design Decisions">
1. **A path for Drafters on mobile (Friction #1).** A Create button in the bottom navigation opens the way to Local Transfer, with a flow that starts by picking the recipient — chosen by 9 out of 10 users in testing.
2. **Drop the batch load (Friction #2).** Single is the main path with no batch name up front; batch opens through the add-recipient icon (Iteration 3, chosen by 6 out of 9 users). Up to 10 recipients, no file upload.
</Section>

<PartHeader number="04" title="Solution" />

<Section title=" ">
Users who used to wait until they could open a laptop can now create a Local Transfer straight from the Jack Mobile App.

> **Local transfers, anytime, anywhere.**
</Section>

<ImageFull mobile src="/images/cs-local-transfer-mobile/placeholder.svg" alt="Final Local Transfer prototype in the Jack Mobile App" caption="Prototype — Local Transfer in the Jack Mobile App." />

<Section title=" ">
For a single transfer: open **Create** → pick **Local Transfer** → pick the recipient → enter the amount → confirm and submit. No batch name, no table.

If it turns out halfway through that they need to send to several people, users just tap the **add-recipient icon** — the flow shifts to batch without starting over. Batch details are only asked for at the end, and before submitting users see a summary with Edit/Delete options per transaction. Batch on mobile is capped at **10 recipients** (the average Portal batch holds 8 transactions) with no file upload — large batches still go through the Portal.
</Section>

<Section title="Reflection">
**What I learned most from this project:** designing for mobile isn't about shrinking a desktop feature. The version closest to the Portal was the one users picked least.

> **Users bring habits from the other apps on their phones — and good design follows those habits.**
</Section>
