<PartHeader number="01" title="Project Snapshot" />

<Section title="Background">
Jack Finance is a business financial services provider offering integrated financial management solutions. One of its main services is ***Local Transfer***, built to help companies manage domestic payments in one place.

Jack runs on two platforms: **Jack Portal (web)** and the **Jack Mobile App**. Their roles weren't balanced: every *Local Transfer* could only be created in the Portal.

Every transaction at Jack passes through three roles: the **Drafter** who creates it, the **Approver** who approves it, and the **Releaser** who releases the funds from the company balance. For *Local Transfer*, the Mobile App only served Approvers and Releasers. The Drafter, the person who starts every transaction, still had to open the Portal. The only thing a Drafter could create from their phone was a *reimbursement*.

This project grew out of the same discovery as [Quick Transfer](/work/quick-transfer/): the finding that 72% of Local Transfer batches held a single transaction. This project came first and answered it on the phone; Quick Transfer followed and answered it in the Portal. Both follow the same principle: **single first, batch as a branch**.
</Section>

<PartHeader number="02" title="Context & Problem" />

<Section title="A. Context Problem">
The first signal was low Mobile App usage. Monthly active users across every mobile module sat at just **31–50 users** a month (April–September 2023). The reason was clear: *drafters*, the people who create transactions, had no reason to open the app.

| Month (2023) | Unique Users |
| --- | --- |
| April | 44 |
| May | 50 |
| June | 47 |
| July | 39 |
| August | 31 |
| September | 41 |

**Fira (my PM)** then found data that made it clear who was losing out the most. From an analysis of transaction patterns from January 2023 to February 2024:

> **72% of Local Transfer batches on Jack Portal held only one transaction.**

In other words, many users, especially SME owners and founders, only needed to send one payment. To do it, they still had to sit at a laptop and go through a flow built for bulk payments.

Fira backed this up by reviewing user feedback and running short interviews with a few clients and internal stakeholders. The result was consistent: **having to use a desktop for a quick, single payment felt like a hassle**.
</Section>

<Section title="B. Current Experience">
Picture a Drafter out of the office who needs to pay a supplier that same day. They open the Jack Mobile App, but there's no button to create a new transaction. The only **Create New** button on Home sits on the *Reimbursement* card. So they wait until they can open a laptop, then go through the Portal's *Local Transfer* flow, built for many recipients, to send to one person.

> **Not on mobile:** the Jack Mobile App had no Local Transfer feature.
>
> **Portal only:** the only way was to open a laptop and go through the batch flow, even for a single recipient.

The screenshot and prototype below show how a Local Transfer was created in the Portal.
</Section>

<Section title="C. The Friction Points">
Those two points became **two friction points** that had to be solved together if the feature was going to get used.

**1. Local Transfer didn't exist on mobile.**

In the Jack Mobile App, the only transaction a Drafter could create was a *reimbursement*, through the *Create New* button on the Reimbursement card. There was no Local Transfer feature, and no general Create button that could lead to one.
</Section>

<ImageFull indent mobile src="/images/cs-local-transfer-mobile/friction-1-home.png" alt="Jack Mobile App Home with Create New only on the Reimbursement card" caption="Friction #1: the only button to create a transaction on Home was Create New on the Reimbursement card." />

<Section title=" " indent>
As a result, for urgent payments like paying a supplier or covering an ad-hoc expense, a Drafter out of the office had to wait until they could open a laptop. Business owners and finance managers who work from their phones every day had no other option.

**2. The Portal treated every transfer as a batch.**

The Portal flow always started as a batch: a batch name, a table or file upload, then bulk validation. Yet 72% of batches held only one recipient.
</Section>

<ImageFull indent src="/images/cs-quick-transfer/friction-1.png" alt="Batch name screen in Jack Portal" caption="A batch name up front." />

<ImageFull indent src="/images/cs-quick-transfer/friction-2.png" alt="Table or Excel upload screen in Jack Portal" caption="A table or Excel upload, no form." />

<ImageFull indent src="/images/cs-quick-transfer/friction-3.png" alt="Bulk validation screen in Jack Portal" caption="Bulk validation." />

<Section title=" " indent>
On a phone, that load weighs even more. A batch-first flow can't be carried over to mobile as it is.
</Section>

<Section title="D. Design Opportunity">
With these two friction points, moving the feature to mobile was only half the job. The question that started the design:

> **How do you make Local Transfer feel natural on a phone for a single transfer, while keeping batch open for the people who still need it?**

The two friction points were tied together. Drafters needed a path on mobile, but the existing flow was built for batch and couldn't be carried over as it was. A shrunk-down Portal flow would put the feature on mobile and still be a hassle for the 72% of cases that only need one recipient.

Dropping batch entirely would push users who occasionally send to several people back to their laptops. The goal was one flow that's fast for a single transfer and can still grow into a batch without starting over.
</Section>

<PartHeader number="03" title="Design Decisions" />

<Section title="Kickoff">
After Fira wrapped up *discovery*, with the data mapped out and the problem statement clear, I joined as the UI/UX Designer. My work ran from exploring user flows and iterating on the UI to usability testing and handoff to mobile engineering.
</Section>

<Section title="Exploration & Validation">
I designed **two end-to-end flow iterations** for testing. Each one was a complete answer to both friction points. They differed in **the order of steps**.

- **Iteration 1:** Pick the recipient first. Batch is decided up front with checkboxes; the number of recipients checked decides single or batch.
- **Iteration 2:** The same order as the Portal. Set the destination and transaction details first.
</Section>

<FigmaEmbed url="https://www.figma.com/proto/ymvw1daaIRX7ZnVo9vGxmO/Local-Transfer?node-id=3829-151975&viewport=161%2C19%2C0.05&t=gG3FZ9d28lRsHvml-1&scaling=scale-down&content-scaling=fixed&starting-point-node-id=3829%3A151975&page-id=343%3A14094" title="Iteration 2: the Portal order" />

<Section title=" ">
Iterations 1 and 2 were tested with **8 internal Jack/Transfez users**: 5 already familiar with the Portal's Local Transfer flow, 3 not. The goal was to find out which flow was preferred and easiest to understand. Participants were internal staff rather than real Drafters, so the results show which flow was easiest to understand, not how Drafters behave day to day.

> **5 out of 8 users picked Iteration 1**, which starts with the recipient. 3 users picked Iteration 2, the Portal order.

Testing settled the order of steps. When batch appears was settled afterwards, in a review that led to Iteration 3. Each shaped a design decision that answers one friction point.
</Section>

<Section title="A. A Path for Drafters on Mobile: From Portal-only to Native on the Phone">
**Friction addressed:** #1, Local Transfer didn't exist on mobile.
</Section>

<Section title="A.1 Entry Point: From the Reimbursement Card to a Create Button" indent>
The first step was giving Drafters a way in. Before, creating a transaction on Home was only possible through the Reimbursement card. Now there's a **Create** button in the middle of the bottom navigation, which opens a choice of transaction types, including Local Transfer.
</Section>

<Section title="A.2 Flow: From the Portal’s Order to “Pick the Recipient First”" indent>
An entry point alone wasn't enough. The next question: should the steps follow the Portal, or follow the pattern of banking and e-wallet apps?

| | Portal order (Iteration 2) | Banking & e-wallet pattern (Iteration 1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single | Transfer details → Recipient | Recipient → Transfer details |
| Batch | Batch details → Recipient → Transfer details | Recipient → Transfer details → Batch details |
| Picked by | **3 users** | **5 users** |

Most users picked the pattern they already knew from banking and e-wallet apps.

Familiarity with the Portal didn't decide it. Of the 5 users who knew the Portal, 3 still picked this pattern; on a phone, being asked for batch details first felt off. The other 2 picked the Portal order to keep it consistent and to know what a batch is for before adding recipients. **For most users, consistency with phone habits mattered more than consistency with the Portal.**
</Section>

<Section title="B. The Batch Load: From Batch-first to Single-first">
**Friction addressed:** #2, the Portal treated every transfer as a batch.

The Portal's batch load couldn't come along to the phone, since 72% of batches held only one transaction. Batch couldn't disappear either.

Iterations 1 and 3 both start from the recipient (Decision A). The difference is **when batch appears**. In Iteration 1, batch is decided up front with checkboxes. In Iteration 3, single is the default and batch only opens through the add-recipient icon.
</Section>

<FigmaEmbed indent url="https://www.figma.com/proto/ymvw1daaIRX7ZnVo9vGxmO/Local-Transfer?node-id=3829-133367&viewport=161%2C19%2C0.05&t=gG3FZ9d28lRsHvml-1&scaling=scale-down&content-scaling=fixed&starting-point-node-id=3829%3A133367&page-id=343%3A14094" title="Iteration 3: batch opened through the add-recipient icon" />

<Section title=" " indent>
Testing confirmed the recipient-first order, but Iteration 1 still asked about batch up front with checkboxes. After reviewing it, **Edo, our CEO**, leaned toward opening straight on the recipient list without checkboxes, so every transfer starts as a single one. The data backed this up: 72% of batches held only one transaction. That became **Iteration 3**. A single transfer carries no batch steps at all, and batch is still there through the add-recipient icon when it's needed.

*Batch became a branch of the single flow instead of a separate path from the start.*
</Section>

<ImageFull indent src="/images/cs-local-transfer-mobile/batch-flow.png" alt="Local Transfer mobile user flow: single by default, batch as an optional branch" caption="User flow: single by default, batch as an optional branch." />

<Section title="Recap: Two Final Design Decisions">
1. **A path for Drafters on mobile (Friction #1).** A Create button in the bottom navigation opens the way to Local Transfer, with a flow that starts by picking the recipient, chosen by 5 out of 8 users in testing.
2. **Drop the batch load (Friction #2).** Single is the main path with no batch name up front; batch opens through the add-recipient icon (Iteration 3, from Edo's review and the 72% single-transaction data). Up to 10 recipients, no file upload.
</Section>

<PartHeader number="04" title="Solution" />

<Section title="Local Transfer on the Phone">
Drafters who used to wait until they could open a laptop can now create a Local Transfer straight from the Jack Mobile App, with single as the default and batch one tap away.
</Section>

<Section title="Final Flow">
For a single transfer: open **Create** → pick **Local Transfer** → pick the recipient → enter the amount → confirm and submit. No batch name, no table.
</Section>

<FigmaEmbed url="https://www.figma.com/proto/9FaFxxDoTxNaEUkl0BDyuF/Study-Case?page-id=402%3A60&node-id=7193-28927&viewport=-774%2C815%2C0.16&t=SEKsuUnJn7uITSbC-1&scaling=scale-down&content-scaling=fixed&starting-point-node-id=7193%3A28927&show-proto-sidebar=1" title="Prototype: Local Transfer in the Jack Mobile App" caption="Prototype of the final Local Transfer flow on mobile." />

<Section title="Key Details">
- **Batch without starting over.** If users realise halfway through that they need to send to several people, they tap the **add-recipient icon** and the flow shifts to batch, keeping what they've entered.
- **Batch details at the end.** Batch details are only asked for at the end. Before submitting, users see a summary with Edit/Delete options per transaction.
- **A cap of 10 recipients.** Batch on mobile holds up to **10 recipients** (the average Portal batch holds 8 transactions) with no file upload. Large batches still go through the Portal.
</Section>

<Section title="Reflection">
**What I learned most from this project:** designing for mobile means rethinking the flow for the phone. Shrinking the desktop feature doesn't work. Even among users who knew the Portal, most picked the phone pattern over the Portal's order. Testing wasn't the last word either: the final flow came from a review after testing, backed by usage data.

> **Users bring habits from the other apps on their phones, and good design follows those habits.**
</Section>
